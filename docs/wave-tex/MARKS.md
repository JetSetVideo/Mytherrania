## Shapes

```ts
type ScarKind = "incision" | "laceration" | "claw" | "puncture" | "burn" | "stitched";
interface Scar {
  kind; region: Region; side?: 0 | 1; anchor: [u, v]; lengthMm; widthMm;
  angle?: number | null;      // rad in the region frame; null = Langer's line at the anchor
  ageDays;                    // stage: inflammatory < 4 d <= proliferative < 21 d <= remodelling < 365 d <= mature
  keloid?: boolean | null;    // null = rolled from ancestry x site x kind (seeded)
  seed; mirror?: boolean;
}
type TattooStyle = "tribal" | "runic" | "sigil" | "band" | "script" | "dotwork";
type InkName = "black" | "blueBlack" | "red" | "ochre" | "woad";
interface Tattoo {
  style; motif: TattooSymbol /* Symbols.ts */; region; side?; anchor: [u, v]; sizeMm;
  angle?: number | null; inks: InkName[] /* [line, accent] */; ageYears; seed; mirror?; facial?: boolean;
}
interface MarkSet { scars: Scar[]; tattoos: Tattoo[] }   // = Appearance.marks (absent -> generated defaults)
```

**Region frame** (Placement.ts). Every (region, side) of the skin is a cylinder: origin, unit axis (v+ = up for
trunk/neck/head/face, PROXIMAL for limbs), front (u = 0), lateral (u > 0 away from the midline; +x on midline
regions), radius, half length. `anchor = (u, v)`, u = angle / pi (wraps), v = axial / halfLength. Millimetres on
the unrolled cylinder: `x = u pi r`, `y = v halfLength`; a mark's `angle` rotates its own (s, t) axes in that plane
(0 = circumferential/transverse, pi/2 = longitudinal). `regionFrames(mesh)` measures the frames (centroid; limb axis
from the segment chain — centroid of the distal neighbour to this one, the covariance's principal axis kept when it
agrees within 0.85 — because a short thick ork deltoid fooled plain PCA into a forward axis; mean radial distance;
95th-percentile axial reach). `nominalFrames(height)` is the same table measured off a 1.8 m human, scaled.
Because skin points map to (x, y) by their BIND-pose position, a mark wraps round the limb and rides the skin as
muscles deform. Paired regions are mirror frames: (u, v) names the same anatomical spot on both sides.

## Medical rationale (plain language)

- **Wound healing has three overlapping phases.** Inflammation (first ~4 days): vessels dilate and leak, the wound
  is red, swollen and moist. Proliferation (~day 4 to week 3): granulation tissue fills the gap, pink and still
  raised. Remodelling (week 3 to one or two years): the collagen is rebuilt denser and aligned, vessels regress, so
  redness fades over months. A mature scar is **paler** than the skin around it (fewer working melanocytes),
  **flat or slightly sunken**, **shiny** (no pores, no hair follicles, no sweat glands) and **hairless**.
  Model: redness `exp(-a/75 d)`, maturity `1-exp(-a/110 d)`, swelling `exp(-a/40 d)`; colour =
  desaturate toward the skin's own luma, multiply by a red tint, screen-lift toward white (all relative to the
  body's skin, so a hypopigmented scar on green ork skin reads pale grey-green, not pink).
- **Older = paler and flatter** (TX43): redness and height never rise with age, lift/desaturation never fall, and
  the scar colour's luma never falls on five skins from pale elf to very dark.
- **Atrophic scars.** Punctures and claw rakes lose tissue and heal sunken (-0.4 / -0.15 mm); a clean surgical
  incision heals flush.
- **Keloids** are scar tissue that keeps growing past the wound's borders for months and then persists: raised
  (plateau 2.5 mm), spreading (x1.6 width), staying dark-pink rather than paling. Real-world rates differ by
  ancestry by an order of magnitude and peak on the chest/sternum, shoulders and ear lobes (an ear-lobe piercing is
  the textbook keloid); palms and soles almost never. The game maps that onto its peoples: orks heal with exuberant
  scar tissue, elves almost never form keloids. Chance = ancestry x site x kind, capped at 0.8.
- **Hypertrophic scars** (added after QD's review Z-24) are the common cousin of the keloid: raised, red and
  firm but staying WITHIN the wound's border, peaking at 3-6 months and regressing over one to two years; deep burns
  heal hypertrophic in roughly 30-70 % of cases, wounds under tension (chest, shoulders, neck) more often. Model:
  activity `b = (a/120) e^(1 - a/120)` (1 at ~4 months, 0.4 at 1 y, 0.04 at 2 y); height `+1.5 b` mm, redness
  `max(r, 0.7 b)`, paling held back by `(1 - b)`; spread 1. Chance = kind (burn 0.5 ... puncture 0.02) x tension site
  x (1 + 2 x the ancestry's keloid propensity); a keloid wins over it. A human chest burn lands at 0.66 (inside
  the literature's 30-70 % band); an ork's at 0.78 (past it, by the same identity choice as the keloids). Monotone (older = paler/flatter) again after
  the peak (TX45).
- **The stage a scar SHOWS** (closing round, F; QD ruling Z-115): `scarStageOf(age, keloid, hypertrophic)` = the stage by
  age, except that a keloid, and a hypertrophic scar while its activity is >= `HYPERTROPHIC_ACTIVE` (0.1, up to ~19 months),
  stay "remodelling": a raised scar is never labelled "mature: pale, flat" (`scarLook().stage`, the creator's stage words, the
  capture HUD). An ORDINARY 400-day laceration is -0.05 mm (flat / slightly atrophic) and paler than the skin around it (TX96-97).
- **Scar colour over the painted skin** (closing, F): `marksToDecals({ skinAlbedo })` composes a scar over the preset's painted
  skin AROUND it (`localSkinSrgb`, 15-50 mm), not over the body's mean skin: the wash / edge / zenithal prime vary across the
  body, and a mature scar composed on the mean read darker than the lighter forearm it sat on.
- **Ork keloid rate is a fantasy identity choice, said plainly:** a laceration on an ork's chest keloids 33 % of the
  time (bloodfang 44 %), against 11 % for a human and 2 % for an elf. Real-world keloid prevalence tops out around
  1 in 6 in the most susceptible populations; orks are deliberately past that ("they heal with exuberant scar
  tissue"), and the generated share is 18 % of ork scars (table below).
- **Langer's lines** (cleavage lines) follow the dermis' collagen grain: horizontal on the chest, sternum, front of
  the abdomen, neck and pelvis; oblique on the flanks and over the scapulae; longitudinal along the long limb
  segments, turning concentric over the joints; transverse on the backs of hands and feet; sagittal on the scalp;
  on the face horizontal across the forehead and otherwise radiating from the orifices (crow's feet, perioral lines).
  Surgeons cut along them; so incisions and stitched wounds default to them. Trauma does not choose: generated
  lacerations and claws lie at any angle, and **a wound across the lines gapes** and heals up to 1.6x wider
  (`gapeFactor`, TX47).
- **Sutures** left in place leave "railroad-track" marks: short transverse bars every ~7 mm, ~4 mm each side of the
  line (stitched kind). **Burns** heal as irregular patches with patchy hyper/hypopigmentation (mottle) and ridged
  contracture bands, slightly less glossy than a line scar.
- **Contrast ceiling (C-MARK):** a scar's albedo is never further (sRGB distance) from the skin than fresh black ink
  at 0.92 opacity would be on that skin (TX48, worst ratio 1.000 = the cap engaged on the darkest skins).
- **Hair:** follicles are destroyed in scar tissue; `sampleScar().hairless` is 1 on the tissue and a 1.5 mm margin.
  `hairlessAt(res, x, y, z, region)` (per follicle) and `hairlessMask(mesh, res)` (per vertex) are the mask the hair
  pass (docs/wave-x/HAIR.md, `HairFollicles`) can read to leave a bald line through a brow or beard; the vertex
  layers also write it as channel `mask:hairless`.

## Tattoos (tattoo-artist notes)

- **Styles**: sigil (a fine-line glyph), tribal (the glyph in bold with a thorn ring in the accent ink), runic (3-5
  glyphs in a row between two rule lines, motif first), band (two border lines and the glyph repeated round the
  whole limb — it wraps with the circumference, TX51), script (a cursive baseline of loops broken into words),
  dotwork (the glyph stippled on a jittered grid, dense on the stroke, a sparse halo). Real fine dotwork uses
  0.3-0.5 mm dots; those are sub-pixel at every game camera and the piece vanished on screen (QD Z-78: max dE 11.9 in
  the face close-up), so the game STYLISES: 0.9 mm dots on a 2.6 mm pitch for a design up to 40 mm, scaled up with
  the design (`dotScale`), an honest departure from the craft for legibility.
- **Motifs** come from `Symbols.ts`: birth sign (void ring, mountain peaks, flame, moon crescent, storm bolt,
  serpent) first, then the class set (Mage star/bolt, Priest sun/star, Necromancer eye/ring, Warrior sword/peaks,
  Archer arrow/crescent, Barbarian fang/claw, Assassin fang/eye, Rogue claw/crescent), then the race set
  (ork fang/claw/bolt, dwarf peaks/ring/sword, elf crescent/star/serpent, human sword/sun/arrow).
- **Inks** by race: orks woad (plant indigo war-blue) with black, red, ochre; dwarves black, blue-black, ochre;
  elves blue-black and woad; humans black and red.
- **Ageing**: ink sits in the dermis; macrophages carry pigment sideways, so lines spread
  (`0.08 + 0.6 (1 - e^(-y/15))` mm) and the piece fades toward a floor per pigment (carbon black most stable, reds and
  earth pigments fastest); old black drifts blue-green. Years are BIOLOGICAL (`years x 80 / lifespan`): an elf's
  200-year-old sigil has aged like a human's 23-year-old one.
- **Placement**: designs wrap with the region frame (a band closes on itself; a forearm sigil curves round the
  arm); `facial` styles (orc tribal, elven dotwork/sigil) are the only tattoos allowed on the face and never over the
  eyes or lips.

## Face zones (fixed after QD's FAIL Z-69)

The first round placed the eye and mouth zones at real-human millimetres (eyes +-32 mm). This game's heads are
stylised: measured on `generateBody(...).plan.face` in the measured face frame, the eyes sit at u ~ 0.18-0.19
(+-66 mm on a 1.8 m human, +-89 mm on a 2 m ork) and the mouth much closer to the eyes than on a real face, so the
old zones missed the real eyes by 30-62 mm. Now:
- `regionFrames(mesh, { face: plan.face, race })` puts the zones ON this head: eyes = ellipses `1.6 x 1.35` eye
  radii around each eyeball centre (socket + lids), mouth = `2.2 x 1.0` eye radii (lips);
- without anchors the race table `FACE_FEATURES_UV` (means over male + female, seeds 7 and 21) is used on the
  frame (nominal frames take the race too: `nominalFrames(h, race)`);
- Langer's radiating face lines use the same centres (`langerFaceOf(frames)`);
- generated face anchors are derived from the zones (`faceSpots`: forehead, both cheeks, chin, glabella) and a
  generated set carries `generated: true`, so the solver MOVES its marks off a zone on the real body (user sets
  stay put and get the issue reported);
- TX55 pins it: 4 races x 2 sexes x 150 seeds on real heads, 189 generated face marks, 0 touching a real zone.

## Placement solver

`solvePlacement(items, frames, { overlapMax = 0.1 })`: items in order (user marks first, `fixed` = never moved);
each footprint (oriented box in frame mm) must stay inside the region's usable `v` band (and `u` band for the
non-wrapping face), be no wider than the limb's circumference, keep clear of the eye and mouth ellipses (face), and
overlap no accepted mark by more than `overlapMax` of the smaller footprint (9x9 samples, wrap-aware). A movable mark
that fails walks a golden-angle spiral of 40 candidates; a fixed one is accepted with its issues reported
(`validateMarks` = the creator's per-mark report: `overlap | outOfRegion | faceFeature | faceNotAllowed |
regionNotAllowed`). Generated sets are solved on nominal frames and then re-validated as fixed marks; a mark still
in trouble loses its mirror or is dropped (TX53: 1000 characters, 0 issues).

## Outputs

- **R's one-call entry**: `marksToFragmentDecals(set, { race, subrace, mesh, face: plan.face, skinSrgb,
  skinRoughness, coveredRegions })` -> `FragmentDecal[]` (= resolve + `marksToDecals`, TX59). Pass the BODY'S OWN skin colour:
  the scar colour is computed relative to it (a mature scar over green ork skin is pale grey-green); the default is a
  human tone. `coveredRegions` (the outfit's garment regions, e.g. `Recipes.GARMENT_REGIONS`) hides marks under
  cloth: they stay in the set, `validateMarks` reports `covered: true` for the creator, and no decal is emitted
  (QD Z-37; `includeCovered` overrides for a bare-skin capture).

- **(b) fragment decals in R's layout** (`marksToDecals(res, { mesh, skinSrgb, skinRoughness })` ->
  `FragmentDecal & { id, priority, wrapAxis? }`, <= `DECAL_MAX` = 16 by salience, the rest listed in `dropped`):
  incision/laceration -> `line`, claw -> `line` (count 3-4, spacing), stitched -> `stitched` (count <= 8, pitch),
  puncture/burn -> `disc`, sigil/tribal/runic -> `glyph` (runic: count + spacing), dotwork -> `dots` (dot radius
  0.9 mm x dotScale, pitch 2.6 mm x dotScale), band -> `band` (tangent = limb axis, wrapRadius), script -> `line`. Albedo LINEAR = the
  capped scar colour over the body's skin (opacity 1) or the aged ink (opacity = faded opacity); roughness = skin x
  stage scale (< 1); height = stage relief in metres (clamped to +-8 mm); blur = 0.3 mm (scar edge) or the ink
  spread. Centres are taken off the skin (weighted 4 nearest vertices of that region/side in frame mm; TX59: worst
  centre-to-skin distance printed). `wrapAxis` (= the limb axis) is an extra for glyph/dots — the brief's
  "wrap about the tangent" would curl an upright forearm glyph the wrong way.
- **(a) per-vertex low-frequency contributions through L's layer API** (`markFields(res)` -> L's `MarkField` per
  id `mark.<id>`; `marksToLayers(res, { dropped })` -> L `Layer[]`): vertices are 22-35 mm apart, so lines live in
  the fragment pass and the vertex layer carries what is broad and would otherwise be double-counted never: the
  perilesional erythema of a fresh wound (multiply, opacity = redness) and of a fresh tattoo (first ~2 weeks), the
  shine (roughness multiply x tissue share), the mean relief (keloid swelling), and `mask:hairless`. A mark whose
  decal fell off the 16 budget also gets a normal-blend tint at its mean coverage so it never vanishes. Each mark also
  has a `stage: "fragment"` twin layer (`mark.<id>.decal`) so the Layers inspector lists and toggles it. TX59 runs
  L's `validateStack` and `evaluateStack` on them.
- **Defaults** (`defaultMarks({ race, subrace, className, birthsign, ageYears, seed, heightM })`, own RNG stream
  `rng32(seed ^ 0x4d41524b)` so no other roll shifts): scars Poisson(race x class x age) capped at 6, kind by class
  (+ race nudge: orks clawed, forge dwarves burned), region by kind (forearms first: defensive wounds), 5-10 % fresh
  wounds for front-liners; tattoos by race/sub-race chance, 1 + Poisson(extra), style/ink/motif by race, dwarf clan
  bands given at majority. `marksForProfile(profile, seed)` = `appearance.marks` (sanitized) ?? the defaults.
- **Saves**: `sanitizeMarks(unknown)` drops unknown kinds/styles/regions/motifs/inks, clamps numbers into
  `MARK_RANGES`, truncates to 12 scars / 8 tattoos, keeps absent optionals absent: a set round-trips JSON and the
  profile library byte-identically (TX57).

## Known limits (honest list)

- The region frame is a cylinder: on the flat of the back or the face the arc-length mapping stretches a design by
  up to ~10 % where the true surface departs from the fitted radius.
- Mirrored glyphs are drawn with the same handedness (R's shader has no flip): asymmetric glyphs (bolt, serpent)
  read rotated rather than reflected on the mirror side.
- Script is a procedural cursive texture, not legible letters; in R's shader it falls back to a `line` decal.
- Scar healing time is the same for every ancestry (only ink ageing scales with lifespan).
